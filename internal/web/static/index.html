Methodological Disclaimer: Calculations are based on spontaneous adverse event reports from the US FDA FAERS database. Disproportionality metrics ($\text{PRR}$, $\text{ROR}$) quantify reporting frequency relative to background rates, not clinical incidence or verified causality. Factors like notoriety bias (Weber effect), stimulated reporting, and lack of prescription denominators must be considered during signal validation.
Statistical Methodology & Mathematical Formulation

Signal detection in pharmacovigilance evaluates whether a specific adverse drug reaction (ADR) is reported more frequently for a target drug than expected based on the background reporting rate across all other medications in the database.

1. Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR)

Compares the proportion of target reaction reports for the drug of interest against all other drugs:

PRR = [a / (a + b)] / [c / (c + d)]

Evans/EMA criterion: PRR ≥ 2.0 with a ≥ 3 and χ² ≥ 4.0.

2. Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR)

Odds of reporting the event with the target drug relative to other drugs:

ROR = (a × d) / (b × c)

Confidence intervals computed via Haldane-Anscombe asymptotic variance.

3. Yates' Corrected Chi-Square

1-degree-of-freedom continuity-corrected test statistic for 2x2 contingency matrices:

χ² = N(|ad - bc| - N/2)² / [(a+b)(c+d)(a+c)(b+d)]
